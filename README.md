# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

This project explores why employees leave the company and what factors contribute most to attrition. I used SQL, Python, and Power BI to analyze the data and turn the findings into clear insights for HR teams.

**Dataset:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition (1,470 employees)

## Key Findings

- The overall attrition rate is **16.12%**.
- Employees who work overtime are much more likely to leave (30.5% vs 10.4%).
- Younger employees (18–25) have the highest attrition rate at **35.77%**. When this group also works overtime, the attrition rate jumps even higher.
- New joiners (0–2 years at the company) leave at a rate of nearly **30%**.
- Employees in the lowest salary range (Below ₹3k) show significantly higher attrition.
- Sales Representatives have the highest attrition among all job roles (almost 40%).
- Single employees leave more often than married or divorced employees.

## Recommendations

- Reduce excessive overtime, especially for younger and newer employees.
- Improve the onboarding experience and support employees during their first two years.
- Review and adjust compensation for lower salary bands where attrition is high.
- Create focused retention plans for Sales roles and the 18–25 age group.
- Work on improving job satisfaction and work-life balance.
- Keep a closer watch on high-risk groups (young + overtime + low salary + short tenure).
